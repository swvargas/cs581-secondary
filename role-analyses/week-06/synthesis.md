## Shared consequence, different decisions

The CCE analysis starts with three potential physical harms: theft/tampering/sabotage following unauthorized protected-area access; injury from unexpected barrier movement or vehicle intrusion; and injury/death from blocked evacuation or delayed emergency response. Observation suppression can conceal unauthorized access, but is not necessary for it. These are engineering priorities, not observed harms or quantified risk rankings.

The two roles share the objective of trustworthy physical protection but make different decisions. A normal HMI, running service or restored coil value is insufficient assurance for either role. The workshop verified active PSI services, loopback listeners, read-only Modbus responses, HMI 401 responses and some recent SIEM receipts—not authenticated HMI operation, independent physical protection or incident recovery.

| Dimension | Incident Response Coordinator | Supply Chain Manager |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate objective | Determine present protective impairment/unknowns, coordinate approved coverage and containment, and assign decision owners while maintaining egress. | Determine which supplier components/support relationships actually exist, whether an affected version or delivery problem is present, and what trusted remediation is available. Do not assume exposure exists. |
| Primary questions | Is a protective function impaired now? What can be isolated safely? Who acknowledges and verifies recovery? | Which exact product/version/artifact was supplied? Are embedded components inventoried? Can support credentials and updates be trusted? |
| Evidence needed, not presumed available | Independent physical contacts or qualified observations; emergency-access behavior; source/receipt times; preserved access/connector records; approvals, acknowledgment and controlled recovery tests. | Supplier/model/version inventory, SBOM, release manifests and trusted build/signature provenance; support agreements, named identities/session approvals, firmware lifecycle and replacement records. |
| Authority boundary | Coordinates security, operations, IAM, network and material control; routes notification assessment to the designated notifier rather than inventing reportability. | Coordinates procurement/vendor remediation and acceptance criteria; does not approve unsafe field changes or certify physical recovery alone. |
| Success criteria, not completed outcomes | Unauthorized path denied where identified, required telemetry retained, physical protection and emergency access independently accepted, unresolved consequences assigned. | Verified product/component applicability, trusted remediation/support process, affected assets remediated or explicitly compensated, and procurement/maintenance acceptance evidence. |

## PSI-specific tradeoff

The received midterm proposal describes a disabled vendor identity whose cached token still worked, but explicitly does not prove equivalent lab cache interfaces. The coordinator must not wait for supplier attribution before protecting the area; the supply-chain manager must not treat a closed AD ticket or removed SSH key as effective revocation across all stores. They jointly require approved test identities, denied reuse and session invalidation evidence.

Likewise, an immediate package upgrade may destroy evidence or disrupt access protection. Preserve the relevant baseline alongside urgent protective action, test the trusted artifact and rollback, obtain operational approval, then implement. Do not delay an urgent life-safety action solely to finish evidence collection; document any resulting evidence loss. Conversely, if impairment is established, “awaiting vendor response” is not a reason to leave it without operationally approved protection. No current physical bypass or established guard coverage was verified by this workshop.

## Module 6 readings applied to the role comparison

### INL CCE: prioritize the consequence, then establish the dependencies

The INL overview begins with critical functions and highest-consequence events, then examines the systems, components and information exchanges that enable them (Freeman et al., 2016, **§§2.1–2.2, pp. 3–4**). This changes the coordinator's priority from “restore the PSI process” to “establish whether access protection and emergency access can be relied on.” Our running services and normal sampled coils cannot answer that physical-function question. The supply-chain manager contributes the dependency evidence: which application artifacts, supporting components and maintenance relationships actually participate in those functions. Missing provenance, device inventory and scenario/lab mapping are therefore analysis gaps, not proof of a compromised supplier.

INL next uses an adversary approach to determine how a particular consequence could be achieved and turns that analysis into mitigations, protections and tripwires (**§§2.3–2.4, pp. 4–5**). Applied to PSI, the coordinator needs detection and response at the consequential action, while the supply-chain manager investigates whether software delivery or supplier access could enable it. For M8, opening an incident on a single high-severity tamper assertion preserves the approximately one-second events we found; independent evidence is still needed to establish their cause and physical significance. **The single-assertion threshold is our proposed PSI design choice, not an INL-prescribed threshold.** The shared CCE lesson is not to substitute self-reported “normal” values for protection: separately trusted command/physical evidence and engineering safeguards must be validated before being credited.

### NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1: manage supplier uncertainty through the life cycle

NIST describes information asymmetry between acquirers and suppliers: the acquirer may lack visibility into how technology is developed, integrated, deployed or supported, and exposure depends on the critical functions that the supplied products/services support (Boyens et al., Rev. 1, updated 2024, **§1.5.2, p. 10**). Our package metadata and hashes identify what was observed, but not an approved origin, a trusted build or the code loaded by a running process. The supply-chain manager should obtain release/provenance and actual supplier-support records; the coordinator uses the uncertainty to request operationally approved protective assurance, not to announce upstream compromise. This also explains why the Armatura advisory cannot establish PSI exposure without an actual product/version and access-prerequisite match.

NIST's examples distinguish malicious insertion from an integrator's reuse of vulnerable code, while its risk discussion separates threats, vulnerabilities, likelihood and impact (**§2.2, pp. 20–22**). This supports our exposure ladder: a vulnerable legitimate supplied product is not the same claim as a compromised supplier, and neither an affected asset nor an unexplained tamper indication proves exploitation. In a disagreement over immediate patching, the coordinator should articulate the consequential function at risk; the supply-chain manager should establish product applicability and a trusted remediation path. They should not resolve uncertainty by either installing an unrelated fix or claiming that no product match means no other risk exists.

NIST treats framing, assessment, response and monitoring as an iterative process requiring collaboration across security, procurement, engineering and other stakeholders (**§2, pp. 18–19**). Section 3 also places C-SCRM throughout acquisition and post-award monitoring, explicitly including open-source and other acquisitions outside formal procurement (**§3.1, p. 37**). Consequently, the supply-chain role must address the Python software/deployment path as well as any purchased physical controller. Its responsibilities continue after an update is obtained: verify agreed requirements and monitor changes. The coordinator supplies incident/acceptance evidence and ensures unresolved consequences have owners. **The joint recommendation is therefore a continuing acceptance loop, not a one-time advisory lookup or a declaration of recovery from a package upgrade.** These are applications of guidance, not claims that the lab has implemented a NIST C-SCRM program.

## Potential disagreements and how to resolve them

| Decision | Potential disagreement | Coordinated resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Restrict supplier/connector access | Coordinator favors prompt containment; supply-chain manager worries about losing diagnostics, vendor support or required telemetry. | Map actual dependencies, preserve evidence where feasible, and have operations/security approve bounded restriction with an independent recovery route. Neither a broad block nor broad reconnection is automatically safe. |
| Patch or replace now | Coordinator seeks a rapid trustworthy baseline; supply-chain manager needs an exact affected product match, authentic release and supported upgrade path. Either may resist delay for different reasons. | Do not patch a merely analogous product. Once applicability is established, combine approved interim protection with tested deployment/rollback and physical-function acceptance. |
| Declare supplier compromise | Incident clues may encourage an early attribution claim; vendor/product evidence may only show a vulnerable legitimate component. | Maintain separate claims for product presence, affected version, access prerequisites, exploitation, upstream compromise and physical impact. Record confidence and provenance for each. |
| Close after service restoration | Availability/operational pressure may favor closure; supply-chain remediation may focus on an installed fix or vendor confirmation. | Require joint technical evidence and operational acceptance. A running service, normal register, signature or upgrade receipt is insufficient; retain unresolved physical/material questions separately. |
| Pay for independent assurance | Immediate response favors temporary observers; procurement may prefer existing platform telemetry or defer investment. | Use authorized temporary measures where needed and plan sustainable independent administration/physical evidence. Another `psi`-controlled collector cannot resolve falsified normal readings. |

## Advisory-driven joint action

Live retrieval of [CISA ICSA-26-274-01, Armatura LLC Armatura One](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-26-274-01) on November 17 UTC found affected releases below 4.7.2 / USA 4.6.1, embedded ActiveMQ CVE-2023-46604 and supplier credential/logging defects. It is an instructive physical-access supply-chain case, **not evidence the Python/Modbus PSI lab contains Armatura or is vulnerable to those CVEs**. A vulnerable embedded dependency also does not prove malicious supplier compromise.

The supply-chain manager first obtains product/component inventory, then checks affected versions and the connectivity/access prerequisites for each flaw. A product/version match identifies an affected asset, not automatically a reachable exploit path, exploitation or supplier compromise. If an affected Armatura deployment is found, official fixes are **4.7.2 / 4.6.1_USA**; obtain the supported path from official technical support. The coordinator translates established exposure into safely sequenced containment, evidence preservation and operational acceptance. Both retain CISA's distinction between exploitation of CVE-2023-46604 in other ActiveMQ deployments and no exploitation specifically targeting Armatura One known to CISA for these findings.

The advisory changes the recommendation rather than merely adding a citation: inventory embedded middleware, check for reusable installation secrets, restrict credential-bearing support artifacts and verify trusted updates. These are proposed adaptations; no Armatura presence, supplier compromise, fix deployment or effective control was demonstrated.

## Coordinated recommendation and open work

1. **Coordinator leads present-state triage:** assign named security/operations/monitoring leads and backups; route the unexplained alarms for review; distinguish claimed occurrence, receipt and discovery times. Operations/security decide any immediate protective measures while preserving emergency access. Do not claim physical impairment or recovery beyond evidence.
2. **Supply-chain manager leads dependency validation in parallel:** obtain approved package/build baselines, actual device/SBOM inventory, supplier support identities and maintenance/expiry records. Current hashes identify observed files, not trusted provenance. Keep the three candidate vectors—artifact substitution, supplier-support abuse, and vulnerable supplied access-control stacks—conditional where prerequisites are unknown.
3. **Jointly approve targeted changes:** confirm actual access/telemetry dependencies and isolate only through authorized plans. Preserve evidence and known-good recovery paths; never automatically re-enable a compromised credential or broad connector exception. Product fixes require an affected match, trusted artifact, tested rollback and operational sign-off.
4. **Jointly commission monitoring:** validate independently administered transaction/receipt monitoring and actual physical evidence sources before crediting them. The primary strategy proposes critical-page delivery within 60 seconds, human acknowledgment within two minutes of delivery and escalation by five minutes from first attempt; life-safety obstruction uses immediate voice action. These targets, roster and failure escalation must be tested, not assumed operational.
5. **Close only on accepted evidence:** record who implemented each change, actual time, test result, limitations and next review. Operations/physical security accept protective function and egress; technical owners verify revocation/required telemetry; the supply-chain role verifies provenance/version/support controls; the coordinator maintains cross-system/material follow-up and routes reportability to the designated notifier. Unknown or unrecoverable historical evidence stays explicit rather than becoming a negative finding.

Together, INL's dependency-to-tripwire approach (**pp. 4–5**) and NIST's iterative risk/monitoring process (**pp. 18–19**) require both roles to revisit the decision when new supplier or incident evidence arrives. Record which protective function a change is intended to preserve, which supplier/dependency evidence supports it, and what independent acceptance result is still missing. That connects immediate consequence reduction to sustainable supplier assurance without treating any proposed control as effective before testing.

All recommendations are proposed responsibilities, not accepted assignments. No Canvas coordination, supplier response, revocation test, installed control, physical-function verification or recovery is claimed here.
